# S03 transaction data and accounting

Source: Chen, D. (2012), [Online Retail II](https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/502/online+retail+ii), UCI Machine Learning Repository, [doi:10.24432/C5CG6D](https://doi.org/10.24432/C5CG6D), [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). Retrieve the official ZIP over HTTPS, at most 60 MB. `config.json` records exact archive and workbook SHA-256 values. ZIP size 45,622,418 bytes; workbook size 45,622,278 bytes. Attribution is preserved; original aggregate analysis does not imply publisher endorsement.

The workbook has 1,067,371 rows in two sheets: 525,461 (December 1, 2009–December 9, 2010) and 541,910 (December 1, 2010–December 9, 2011). Actual fields are Invoice, StockCode, Description, Quantity, InvoiceDate, Price, Customer ID and Country. Price is nominal pounds sterling. The source is a UK non-store giftware retailer with many wholesale buyers. It is not a representative retail panel.

The sheets **overlap in December 2010**. There are 22,202 distinct complete row values present in both sheets, 6,865 within-sheet duplicate rows in the first sheet and 5,268 in the second. Primary exact-row deduplication leaves 1,033,036 rows. A prespecified multiset-union sensitivity retains the maximum observed multiplicity of each row across the two sheets: 1,044,848 rows, including 11,812 repeated lines. This separates duplicate-sheet copies from possible legitimate same-item repetitions; no reliable line identifier resolves the latter.

After primary deduplication, 235,151 rows lack Customer ID and 4,275 lack Description. Missing IDs cannot be reconstructed or treated as a single customer. No description is used in models. There are 19,104 cancellation-prefixed rows, 22,496 nonpositive quantities and 6,019 nonpositive prices; these groups overlap. One cancellation-prefixed row has positive quantity. Raw quantity ranges from −80,995 to +80,995; anomalous and cancelled orders make gross-versus-net accounting consequential.

Primary purchase lines require a known ID, strictly positive quantity and price, and no C-prefixed invoice. Aggregate all eligible invoices for a customer on a calendar day to one purchase occasion. There are 779,425 eligible lines, 5,878 customers, 36,969 invoices and 33,107 purchase days, totaling £17,374,804.268 before rounding. Every eligible invoice belongs to one customer and date. Daily aggregation avoids treating same-day invoice splits as additional continuous-time purchases.

A separate net-spend sensitivity uses all known-ID, positive-price signed quantities, including cancellation/return lines. Its full-source sum is £16,289,991.288; negative lines total −£1,084,812.98. Credit invoices do not reliably identify the original purchase, so returns cannot be assigned back to original transaction dates. Gross positive purchasing is the primary modeled outcome; it is never described as profit or net realized revenue.

Only customer IDs observed before a cutoff and with at least 30 days since first observed purchase are forecast. First observed purchase is not a verified acquisition date. Customer IDs, invoice IDs, line records and individual predictions remain outside Git/publication. Publish aggregate cohorts with at least 50 customer-cutoff observations, plus suppression and dependence limitations. Country and customer ID do not enter predictors.
